# ADR-001: Engagement Methodology

**Status:** Accepted

## Background

Hunt needed a starting position and a scope before any attack work began.
Atlas provides four hosts and a working AD, Sentinel provides monitoring
across all of them, but nothing had yet defined how Hunt would actually
approach the environment, or what "done" looks like for this engagement.

## Decision

This is an internal, assumed-breach assessment. The attacker already has
network access to corpnet through KALI01, and the engagement focuses on what
that access can turn into: enumeration, credential capture, privilege
escalation and lateral movement toward DC01.

Initial access uses LLMNR/NBT-NS poisoning: KALI01 runs Responder on
corpnet, capturing a credential hash from normal Windows broadcast
name-resolution traffic generated by WS01.

Scope for this engagement is the Windows/AD attack chain, WS01 through DC01.
APP01 is not a target for this pass.

## Why this decision?

A true external, black-box test doesn't really apply here. Corpnet has no
internet-facing perimeter to attack from outside; the closest thing to a
perimeter is KALI01's own dual-homed position, and it's already inside the
network by design. An assumed-breach internal assessment matches how
corpnet is actually built, and it's also how most real internal penetration
tests are scoped: you're rarely testing "can someone get in from the
internet," you're testing "what happens once someone already has."

LLMNR/NBT-NS poisoning was chosen for initial access specifically because it
needs no planted vulnerability. Those protocols are enabled by default on
Windows, and poisoning them to capture credential hashes is one of the most
common real openings on a flat internal network, corpnet included. Starting
here means the first phase of the attack chain is a genuine finding, not a
staged one.

Scoping to WS01 and DC01 keeps this engagement focused on the interesting
attack surface. The AD attack chain, credential theft through Kerberos
abuse through domain compromise, is where the depth is. Extending equal
scope to APP01 now would mean splitting effort across a Linux-specific
assessment that doesn't share much technique with the AD side, better run as
its own focused Hunt iteration later than diluted into this one.

## Trade-offs

Skipping APP01 means this engagement doesn't produce a complete picture of
the environment's security posture. That's an accepted gap for this pass,
not an oversight. A four-host environment doesn't need every host assessed
in the same engagement to produce a meaningful result, and a focused AD
attack chain is more useful as a first Hunt engagement than a shallow pass
across everything.

## Outcome

The attack chain runs Responder on KALI01 against WS01, uses whatever
credential comes out of that to enumerate the domain, and proceeds toward
DC01. Escalation strategy is recorded separately in
[ADR-002](002-planted-misconfiguration.md), since Atlas's AD had nothing
to escalate through as built.
